# post-hack-leverage

## Top rules
1. The 72 hours after the event are worth more than the prize. People remember you now; in two weeks they won't.
2. Follow up with everyone who gave you time: judges, sponsor engineers, mentors, organizers.
3. Post publicly, win or lose. A clean post about a lost hackathon still builds the portfolio.
4. Thank the sponsor by name and tag them. That's the thing they wanted from sponsoring.
5. Decide within a week: archive, portfolio piece, or keep building. Don't let it rot undecided.

## When to use
- Judging has ended (results known or pending).
- Weeks later, when deciding whether to revive a hackathon project.

## Inputs needed
- Project name, live URL, video, repo, Devpost link.
- Result (won what, or didn't place).
- List of people met: name, role, company, what you talked about, contact handle.
- Your goal: internship, startup, network, portfolio.

## Steps

### 1. Contact log (30 min, within 24 h)
| Name | Role / company | What we discussed | Channel | Follow-up sent |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 2. Follow-up messages (send within 48 h)
Template — sponsor/judge:
```
Hi <name>, thanks for <specific thing they said or did at <event>>. We built <project> — <one line>. Here's the 2-min demo: <link>. <One specific question or ask tied to their work.> Would love to stay in touch.
— <your name>
```
Template — mentor:
```
Hi <name>, your tip about <specific> saved us at <moment>. We ended up <result>. Demo: <link>. Thank you.
```
Never send a generic "nice to meet you". The specific line is the whole message.

### 3. Public post (within 72 h)
LinkedIn / X structure:
1. Hook: result or the most surprising thing learned (one line).
2. What we built, one sentence, for whom.
3. The wow moment as a GIF or 30-second clip.
4. One honest lesson (technical or strategic).
5. Thanks: tag team, sponsors, organizers.
6. Link to demo / repo.
Keep it under 150 words for LinkedIn readability.

### 4. Portfolio packaging
- Repo: README with GIF at the top, live link, "Built at <event> in <hours>h, <result>".
- Pin the repo on GitHub.
- Add to personal site/resume: one line with a number ("Won Best Use of X among 120 teams").
- Keep the live deploy up for at least 3 months, or replace with the video if it costs money.

### 5. Keep building? Decision rules
Continue only if two of these are true:
- A real user asked to use it after the event.
- A sponsor/judge offered a concrete next step (pilot, intro, credits, interview).
- It fits a venture or research direction you already have.
- You'd still want to work on it if no one ever saw it.
If continuing: write a one-page plan with a 2-week goal. If not: archive with a clean README.

## Outputs
- Contact log with follow-up status.
- Sent messages.
- Public post draft.
- Portfolio entry text.
- Continue/archive decision.

## Worked example
VoiceViva won the vector-DB sponsor prize, did not place overall.
- Messages to the vector-DB DevRel (asked about per-student index scaling), two judges, one mentor.
- LinkedIn hook: "We lost the main prize and still got the best feedback of the year: 'make it interrupt less.'"
- Decision: continue; two coaching-centre teachers asked for access, and it plugs straight into an existing ed-tech product as a viva mode. Plan: 2-week pilot with one batch.

## Failure modes
- Waiting for "the right time" to post. Engagement dies after 3 days.
- Generic thank-you DMs.
- Taking the deploy down and leaving a dead link on the resume.
- Silently continuing a project with no defined goal.

## Hand-off
New hackathon → `hack-recon`. Project continues → normal product work.
